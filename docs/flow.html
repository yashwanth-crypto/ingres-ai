The three ways it stops early

All of them are reached by design, and none shows an unverified answer as though it were verified.

  • Refusal. The question is not about Punjab groundwater, or names a district the data does not cover. It says what it does cover instead of guessing.
  • The data itself. Either the model produced nothing parsable three times running, or a figure survived the rewrite still unsupported. The retrieved rows and passages are shown raw, marked unverified — a model that rambled has not made the data worse.
  • Rewrite, then re-check. A draft with a problem gets exactly one more attempt, and the result is checked again from scratch. If the rewrite introduces a fault the original did not have, the original is kept: the advisory reviewer must not be able to veto a correct answer.

Why the split at retrieval matters

The database knows how much water there is. It holds no water-quality data at all. So a question about drinking safety goes to the report, and a question about a level goes to SQL — and a numeric question never goes to the report, because retrieving prose about a figure loses the station and the date that make the figure checkable.

What the model is never allowed to do

It does not supply a number, choose what data to fetch, do the arithmetic, decide whether a chart appears, or have the final say on whether an answer is sound. Given a field called projected_year it once cited the field name as a source, so fields the checks need are withheld from it entirely.
